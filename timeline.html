Agnes

A company makes a big bet. Years later it fails, and nobody can say what was actually known at the time. Agnes writes down what has to be true for a bet to work, then keeps checking it against evidence as the evidence arrives.

Validate
Write down every condition the bet depends on, and score each one against dated evidence — for, against, or nothing known.
Why it matters. Strategy documents list goals, not the conditions underneath them. If nobody writes down what must be true, nobody notices when it stops being true.
Simulate
Break one of those conditions on purpose and follow what it costs under each different way of making the same bet.
Why it matters. The same failure costs wildly different amounts depending on how you structured the bet. Owning seven thousand houses and referring buyers to a partner fail very differently.
Monitor
Every time something new is published, recompute — and raise a warning the moment the case changes.
Why it matters. The evidence that kills a bet arrives slowly, inside ordinary quarterly filings. Nobody re-reads the original reasoning each time a new one lands.
The rule that makes this honest: Agnes only ever sees what was published on or before the date you are looking at. Nothing from later. Move the date and she recomputes from scratch — so you see what could genuinely have been known at the time, not what we know now.
