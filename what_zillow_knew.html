What Zillow Knew

On 10 February 2021, Zillow was scaling a bet that rested on six things being true. Six months later, the evidence against it had grown by 80% — and not one thing about how the decision was being managed had changed.

+80% growth in the weight of evidence against the bet between February and August 2021. Over the same period: no gap closed, no guardrail added, no blind spot filled.

Zillow's own filings say nothing about forward price forecasting — not in February, not in August. Every piece of evidence bearing on it comes from outside the company.

We've determined the unpredictability in forecasting home prices far exceeds what we anticipated. Rich Barton, announcing the closure of Zillow Offers — 2 November 2021

Two further readings. In February, the world's warning on throughput (−2, from the Federal Reserve Beige Book's report of labour shortages and material delays) was stronger than anything Zillow had disclosed (−1). And liquidity and working capital show silent in both world columns — those are balance-sheet facts only the company can report. The model does not pretend otherwise.

What is not claimed

The limits, stated plainly

  • Not that Agnes saw what Zillow could not. Zillow held vastly more data than this graph. The company was silent publicly — that is not evidence it never measured forecast error internally.
  • Not that anyone would have acted differently, or that pausing would have saved the money. Both unfalsifiable.
  • Not that the architecture predicts anything. One case, with no matched success, cannot separate "detects deterioration" from "pessimistic about everything".
  • Not that the strategy comparison works. It scores risk with no upside term, so it can only ever prefer inaction. Found by this project's own sensitivity test, and left documented rather than patched with an invented number.
  • Not that the world panel is complete. Six indicators were declared relevant; two carry no data at all, and the graph reports its own blind spots rather than hiding them.
